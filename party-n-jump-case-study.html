Case Study · Party-N-Jump, Copperas Cove TX

Your ERS website should be as easy to use as your rentals are fun.

Same ERS system. Better website experience.

Party-N-Jump runs on Event Rental Systems (ERS) — a solid booking backend with a dated, hard-to-navigate front end. We rebuilt the customer-facing experience on top of it: no ERS migration, no lost bookings, just a site people can actually use.

The starting point

What we found on the live site

party-n-jump.info, like most ERS storefronts, inherits the same handful of structural issues out of the box.

This audit is based on the live page's actual content and markup structure — not a visual crawl — so it reflects real information-architecture and accessibility issues rather than a claim about exact on-screen measurements.

Accessibility gaps

  • ✕  No skip-to-content link
  • ✕  No landmark structure (main/nav/footer)
  • ✕  Category images with no descriptive alt text

Mobile friction

  • ✕  Header logo, phone and CTA repeated twice in the markup
  • ✕  20+ nested nav links with no mobile-specific grouping
  • ✕  Deposit & delivery policy buried in one paragraph

Confusing UX

  • ✕  18+ flat category tiles, no grouping or hierarchy
  • ✕  Full nav list repeated identically in the footer
  • ✕  No single, consistent path from browsing to booking

What the numbers point to

  • 1 Header content (logo, phone, CTA) rendered twice — confusing for screen readers, wasted space on mobile
  • 2 Deeply nested category links with no visual or semantic grouping
  • 3 Flat image grid — no cards, no descriptions, no per-item call to action

What replaced it

  • ✓ Single header, sticky, with one clear Book Now action
  • ✓ 5 organized categories instead of 20+ flat links
  • ✓ Structured cards with headings, descriptions and hierarchy
